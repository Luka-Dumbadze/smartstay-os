**[O]** These values were extracted directly into [P1 Kakheti CSV](../text/P1-pmc-hpi-2026-08.kakheti.csv), with the original source line, source SHA-256, and page retained. They are changes in a price index; the percentages are not occupancies, margins, or changes in realized room revenue. [A01–A02]

**[D]** The same page reports a Kakheti **5-star advertised room-price average of 684 GEL** for August 2026. That is a category-specific quoted price, not a representative boutique-hotel ADR. Its methodology collects standard double-room prices from Booking.com, using a stratified sample of **568 hotels and 371 guesthouses** across **10 regions and Tbilisi**. Average prices use available hotels in the current period; the index uses available hotels in current and base periods. [A02]

**[I] Interpretation.** The regional aggregate conceals different monthly movements: the star-hotel group fell slightly while guesthouses increased. These data support examining realized rates and costs by property type. They cannot establish that an individual hotel's margin improved or that service failures caused lost revenue. [A01–A02]

**[D/O] Source discrepancy that must survive synthesis.** Page 2 says that both spending and average transaction values increased across Kakheti hotels and restaurants. ## 2. Practitioner testimony and operating pressure

**[D] Attribution boundary.** BMG identifies Ketevan/Keti Amirbari as managing partner of Amirbari Hospitality Agency and Shalva Alaverdashvili as a hotelier and Hotels Federation founder. The following are exact sentences in the harvested BMG article paragraphs. They establish the publisher's attributed account, not independent verification of all underlying hotel events. The two Amirbari articles share a speaker and are not independent survey observations. [A10–A14]

### Retention and seasonal reductions

**[D] V1, “Staff Shortages and Seasonality Challenge Kakheti Hotels,” published 7 September 2026, paragraph 1:**

> She said finding and retaining employees is particularly difficult in the region, with many workers moving to Tbilisi or abroad.

**[D] V1, paragraph 2:**

> Seasonality further complicates the problem, as hotels often have to reduce staff after the peak season. This creates an unstable job market and encourages employees to seek more secure opportunities elsewhere, Amirbari said.

**[D] V1, paragraph 3:**

> High staff turnover also affects service quality, making it harder for hotels to build a strong corporate culture and employee loyalty.

**[D] V1, paragraph 4, a counterpoint:**

> Boutique, conceptual and high-end hotels are making efforts to retain their management and staff, helping them maintain service standards despite the region’s labor and seasonal challenges.

**[I] Synthesis.** The reported mechanism is seasonal reductions and insecure work contributing to retention problems, with turnover affecting service quality. The counterpoint matters: the article also describes active retention efforts, so it does not support a blanket assertion that boutiques are incapable of maintaining standards. “Staff reductions” is the source wording; the employment/legal mechanism of those reductions is not specified. [A10–A12]

**[U]** No turnover percentage, seasonal headcount reduction, training cost, failed-handover count, or service-recovery cost is supplied in these paragraphs. A handover-breakdown hypothesis is not an observed incident. [A10–A12]

### Payroll, costs, margins, and exit pressure

**[D] V2, “Many Hotel Owners Want to Sell Their Properties” — Shalva Alaverdashvili, published 4 September 2026, paragraph 3:**

> As competition intensifies, hotels are struggling to generate sufficient revenue, while payrolls and other operating costs have risen sharply over the past three years. As a result, profit margins that once made the sector attractive to investors have largely disappeared.

**[D] V2, paragraph 2:**

> He told BMGTV’s “Tsertili” that he had met with two hotel owners just before the interview, both of whom asked for help selling their properties and were ready to put them on the market after the tourist season ends in late October.

**[D]** The same paragraph says he manages **nine hotels in Tbilisi and the regions**. That is the stated portfolio context for his observations, not the denominator of a representative survey. His account of **two owners** is an anecdote about sale intentions, not a count of completed sales or Kakheti-wide distress. [A13]

**[I] Synthesis.** The account points to pressure between revenue, payroll/other expenses, and owner returns. It does not isolate the amount attributable to avoidable operational waste. Automation savings, EBITDA percentages, and recoverable GEL losses cannot be calculated from the testimony. [A13]

### Demand differs across regions and property concepts

**[D] V3, “Hotel Occupancy Rises, but Trends Vary by Region — Keti Amirbari,” published 2 September 2026, paragraph 3:**

> Kakheti’s hotel market is driven largely by wine tourism and gastronomy. While overall demand has increased, Amirbari says some hotels have failed to reach their full potential.

**[D]** V3 paragraph 1 says performance varies by region, hotel concept, and audience. V1 paragraph 3 describes events, international gatherings, and harvest activity as Kakheti demand occasions. These are reported qualitative demand characteristics, not occupancy estimates. [A11, A14]

**[I] Synthesis.** A common regional demand headline should not be used to assume that all properties face the same operating calendar or demand mix. That inference is grounded in the named practitioner's distinctions, but the corpus does not quantify each segment's workload. [A11, A14]

> Inadequate Data Collection: Limited and inconsistent data reporting hinders performance assessment and decision-making.

**[U] Unsupported candidates excluded from the findings catalog:** billing/folio disputes, missed housekeeping handoffs, late room readiness, double bookings, duplicate refunds, and quantified guest-request response failures. The harvested sources do not document identifiable incidents of those types. Their absence here is not evidence that they never occur; it means they cannot be presented as empirically discovered in this dataset. [Corpus: G1, G2, T1, P1, P2, V1–V3]

**[I] Software implication, not a proven product case.** The supported findings justify investigating continuity of operational responsibilities, trustworthy shared information, and preventable effort/cost inside hotels. They do not yet justify a particular agent topology, a billing automation product, or a savings claim. An AI-native OS/A2A opportunity would require a real cross-responsibility incident and evidence that controlled software actions can alter its outcome. This inference is bounded by the bottlenecks and missing incident evidence above; no hardware or IoT intervention is proposed. [A10–A18]
