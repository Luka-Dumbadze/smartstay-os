The most recent comparable one I can describe is an enquiry received on **8 September at 09:00**, for a December retreat: 16 rooms for two nights, 32 delegates, a meeting room, two coffee breaks and a wine dinner.

We sent an authorized offer for **USD 9,600 gross on 9 September at 15:00**, 30 hours later. The client accepted the approved three-wine menu and our standard 30% deposit. We identified the cellar capacity issue early, and they accepted two tasting groups of 16. There was no conflicting restaurant commitment, and I could approve the room discount within my authority.

I don’t have the correspondence preserved, so I can’t show or quote those exchanges. The available fictional booking-record excerpt, with identifying details omitted, reads:

> Signed 10 September; USD 2,880 deposit recorded 11 September. Event not yet delivered; deposit is not earned revenue or profit.

Our existing worksheet helped make the capacity and approval questions visible. But this was also an easier negotiation: fewer rooms, different dates and no payment exception requiring the owner. It went well in the sense that we secured a signed booking and deposit; the event is still ahead of us. I wouldn’t attribute that result to response speed alone.
